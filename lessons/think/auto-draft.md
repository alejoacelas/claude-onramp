## Thinking is hard

Thinking is hard.

Dump as much thinking as you can into Claude.

Give it all the thinking you've already done, but be really wary of any friction, where you find yourself trying to do more thinking before handing to the AI. Thinking is effortful and our brains would rather do less of it.

I don't want to think you see, I want to get things done. To the extent I hit my desired quality standard through mindless execution, so much the better.

Most things I delegate to Claude are not worth the extra effort. Not only is Claude infinitely patient to my ramblings, it can often notice gaps, etc, etc.

## Talk to it

Some people (most people) can think through talking, just remember the last time you had a conversation about a work task. So you should use that medium.

Talking is a much better format for incomplete thinking. It's surprising how often you can stare at a blank Google Docs, and when a colleague asks you what you're trying to write, you can simply spout the thing you were meaning to say. And it might be worse and more imprecise than what you would have written directly, but again, Claude is very forgiving of those.

Talking is easier. I've done AI workshops running past 3 hours, and find myself struggling to shut up and let people work with their Claudes.

We're already used to relationships of this kind: therapists, friends, and often managers, can serve as external props, processing our half-baked ideas into a plan for action. Claude, lacking deep context of you, may not do as well as any of them, but you should definitely give it as much as it can take.

Claude is a really perceptive listener, so most of the time, you might as well talk to it.

You talk to AIs when you don't know what you need. Sometimes, when getting started, you only know how to specify the outcome: “I want to be able to work while residing in the UK, legally”.

## Incomplete thinking, and doing things poorly

It used to be that thinking needed to be complete to be useful.

Full-loop thinking, where you want to have all the context and information yourself.

Incomplete thinking, where you discharge some (often most) of the responsibility to your AIs.

Makes the difference between doing something poorly/badly/mediocre [is there a word for ‘not up to your usual standards'?] and not doing it at all.

Many things we'll only do if we allow ourselves to do them poorly.

Your execution will move towards things you can do poorly and things you need to excel at.

The graphical interface for this page is something I half-assed. I spit out a quick draft of this EA Forum post, and my whole blog is an ode to poor execution.

Doing things poorly often leads to doing more things, and accumulating poor things is its own kind of excellence.

Good enough. And please lower your standards.

## What thinking to keep for yourself

To get the most of AI, you have to answer a really hard question: what's the thinking you need to do *yourself* to achieve your goals. This is another reason to do things now.

Engage with some things deeply. The ratio depends on the kind of work that you do, but there will be attributes (maybe career planning, strategy, charisma, a specific area of expertise) where you really want to double down and think really hard, plenty of it for yourself.

For some things, you've already done all the thinking needed, and you want to do absolutely zero thinking on them going forward. These should be handled by clean automations.

Approving permissions mindlessly is me delegating that thinking to the machine. Why do I do that:

- the machine knows better what's dangerous and what's not
  - I've done some previous thinking to figure that out. Cached thinking.

You'll find there's a bunch of places in your life where you'd rather think less. And what do you do with your spare capacity? You think more! About the things you care about [the things where your spare thinking can drive the most value now]. (point to use more AI session)

Start a blog! Figure out if veganism has insidious long-term health consequences! Revamp the EA intro fellowship's curriculum! Read The Bible! Think about what's the highest upside version of your project, and get feedback from mentors and colleagues.

## Push until it fails

Do the thinking only when such thinking cannot be done by Claude. How do you know where the limits are? You have to keep pushing until you watch it fail, then, if the task is worth it, figure out (or have Claude figure out) why it failed.
